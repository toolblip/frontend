There's a quiet revolution happening in the developer toolspace. Desktop apps that took minutes to install, required system permissions, and left痕迹 on your machine are being replaced by something simpler: **tools that run right in your browser**.

No downloads. No updates. No "which version do I have again?" Just open a URL and get to work.

## Privacy By Design

When you use a desktop tool—say, a JSON formatter with an auto-decode feature—your data often passes through the app's processing engine, which may phone home, check for updates, or log usage. With browser-based tools, your data stays on your machine. Literally. The processing happens in your tab, on your device, with no server round-trip for most operations.

That's not just marketing copy. It's how the web platform works now. WebAssembly, browser APIs, and client-side JavaScript have gotten *fast*. Really fast.

## Speed of Access

Want to format some JSON while on a call? Open a new tab. Share a screenshot with annotations? Browser. Convert a Base64 string? One keystroke in a URL bar.

Compare that to "let me install this app, restart my machine, find where the icon ended up, and hope the version I got isn't the beta that crashes on Big JSON."

Browser tools load in milliseconds. They scale with your device. And they work exactly the same on a $300 Chromebook as on a maxed-out MacBook Pro.
