Let's be honest — most developer toolkits are built around the same five operations that keep showing up, day after day. You format JSON, you encode Base64, you test a regex, you figure out what `*/5 3 * * 2` actually means, and you generate a hash when you need to verify something. These aren't glamorous tasks. But they're constant.

Here are the five you should have bookmarked.

**1. JSON Formatter** — Raw JSON is unreadable. A good formatter takes that cramped wall of text and makes it scannable, indented, and actually debuggable. Bonus points if it validates at the same time.

**2. Base64 Encoder/Decoder** — Whether you're dealing with API tokens, image data URIs, or just trying to figure out what a weird string is supposed to be, Base64 comes up constantly. A fast encoder/decoder that runs locally in the browser is worth its weight in debugging time.

**3. Regex Tester** — Regex is powerful but brutal to write blind. You need real-time matching against test strings, clear highlighting of capture groups, and a way to share the pattern with a teammate. That's not something you can do reliably in your head.

**4. Cron Expression Parser** — Speaking of cryptic strings — cron expressions are the universal language of "I'll figure this out later." A parser that tells you exactly when `0 9-17 * * 1-5` fires is genuinely useful, especially when you're setting up scheduled jobs and don't want to wait until tomorrow to find out you got it wrong.

**5. Hash Generator** — MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256 — you need to generate one, verify one, or just check if a file matches its checksum. Doing it in-browser without uploading anything is the right way.
