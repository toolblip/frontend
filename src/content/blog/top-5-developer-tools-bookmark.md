There are roughly a billion developer tools on the internet. Most of them are bloated, slow, paywalled, or straight-up malicious. After years of bookmarking and abandoning tools, here's the short list of what actually belongs in your browser's bookmark bar.

## 1. JSON Formatter

You have JSON. It's 47 levels deep, minified into a single unreadable line, and you need to figure out why your API call is failing. A good JSON formatter does three things: pretty-prints the content, validates syntax, and lets you collapse/expand nodes. Bonus points if it highlights errors in place.

## 2. Base64 Encoder/Decoder

Base64 comes up constantly — encoding images for data URIs, working with API tokens, debugging authorization headers. The browser-based versions are fast, free, and run entirely locally. No uploading your potentially sensitive payloads to some random website.

## 3. Regex Tester

Regular expressions are famously write-once, read-never. A good regex tester shows you matches in real-time as you type, explains what each part of your pattern does, and handles different regex flavors. Some even generate code snippets for your language of choice.

## 4. Cron Expression Parser

Cron is cryptic by design. `*/15 9-17 * * 1-5` — sure, makes total sense. A cron parser takes that gibberish and tells you exactly when it runs: "Every 15 minutes, 9 AM to 5 PM, Monday through Friday." If you write or debug scheduled jobs, this saves real time.

## 5. Hash Generator

MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256, SHA-512 — sometimes you need to verify a download, check a password hash, or just generate a fingerprint of some content. A good hash tool handles text, files, or both, and shows results instantly.

## The common thread

All five of these tools share two traits: they're dead simple, and they're used constantly. That's the sweet spot. Not the flashy 50-feature powerhouse you'll never fully learn — just the boring utilities you reach for multiple times a week.

Bookmark them. You'll thank yourself on the next late-night debugging session.
