There's a reason more developers are ditching their downloaded utility apps and heading to the web instead. Browser-based tools aren't just convenient — they're fundamentally better for a lot of the work we do every day.

**Privacy by default.** When you run a tool locally in your browser, your data never leaves your machine. No uploading to some server, no terms of service you didn't read, no risk of your API keys or sensitive payloads sitting in someone else's log. For anyone handling proprietary code, customer data, or just things they'd rather keep private, this is huge.

**Zero setup friction.** Download a desktop app and you might be staring at an installer, a permission prompt, or a "please update to version 3.2.1" nag screen. With browser tools, it's: open tab, do the thing, close tab. No versions to manage, no OS compatibility to worry about, no "requires macOS 13+." Works on your laptop, your work desktop, your friend's machine, or your phone.

**Instant results.** Because everything runs client-side, there's no server round-trip to wait for. A JSON formatter that works locally processes your data in milliseconds. An image optimizer that doesn't upload doesn't have the overhead of transfer time. Speed matters when you're iterating.

**Collaboration made simple.** Share a URL. That's it. No file attachments, no version confusion, no "I sent you the old version." When your tools live in the browser, sharing a result or a tool state is as easy as copying a link.

**Always available, everywhere.** Can't install software on a restricted work machine? Using someone else's computer? On a tablet? A browser tool doesn't care. As long as you have a browser and a connection (or even just a browser, for fully offline tools), you're good.

The web platform has matured enough that browser-based tools can now match — and sometimes beat — native apps in capability. We're only going to see more of this.
