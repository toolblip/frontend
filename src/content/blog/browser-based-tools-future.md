There's a quiet revolution happening in how developers use tools. The shift isn't loud or flashy — it's happening one tab at a time.

**Privacy-first by design.** When you run a tool in your browser, your data never leaves your machine. No servers, no logs, no "we promise we don't store this." Upload a JSON file to debug it? It stays on your device. Paste something sensitive into a Base64 encoder? It evaporates the moment you close the tab. That's not a feature — it's just how the web works when you build it right.

**No install. No updates. No friction.** Desktop apps demand installation, updates, disk space, and often a restart. Browser tools? Open a tab, paste your input, get your output. For tools you use a few times a week — a regex tester, a cron expression parser, a hash generator — installing a native app feels absurd. Your browser is already open anyway.

**Speed that native apps can't touch.** Well, sometimes. For most tasks, a well-built browser tool responds in milliseconds. There's no app launch time, no splash screen, no loading spinner from a bundled Electron app. The tool is just *there*, ready.

**Sync everywhere, automatically.** Your bookmarks, your history, your tabs — they're already everywhere you are. A tool you rely on at your desk is the same tool you use on a borrowed laptop. No iCloud sync, no migrations, no "sorry that feature's only on Pro tier."

**The trade-offs are real, but shrinking.** Browser tools have historically suffered from limited offline support and weaker performance for heavy tasks. But with modern browser APIs, service workers, and WebAssembly, those gaps are closing fast. An image optimizer running in your browser can now rival desktop software — and it still respects your privacy.

The future of developer tooling isn't a bigger download. It's a shorter URL.
