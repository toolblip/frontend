There's a quiet revolution happening in developer tooling. The apps you used to download, install, and update are increasingly living inside a browser tab. And honestly? That's a good thing.

**Privacy first.** When you run a tool locally, your data often passes through someone else's server — or at least touches the internet in ways you can't fully control. Browser-based tools like Toolblip process everything on your device. Your JSON never leaves your machine. Your passwords, your API keys, your sensitive payloads — they stay exactly where they should be: with you.

**No install, no drama.** Remember waiting for a 200MB installer to finish? Fighting with PATH variables? Dealing with "this app requires macOS 12.3 or later" when you're on 12.2? Browser tools kill all of that. Open a tab, paste your data, get your result. It works on a Chromebook, an old laptop, a work machine where you can't install software. It just works.

**Speed that surprises you.** Web apps have a reputation for being slow. That's outdated. Modern browser-based tools are blazing fast for most use cases — especially utility work like formatting JSON, encoding Base64, or generating hashes. You're not waiting for a GUI to render or a native app to cold-start. The overhead is essentially zero.

**Always current.** No more "you're running an outdated version" warnings. The tool updates itself every time you open it. You always have the latest features and security patches without lifting a finger.

**The tradeoff is real but shrinking.** Native apps still win for heavy, sustained workloads — video editing, 3D rendering,编译-intensive tasks. But for the daily grind of formatting, converting, hashing, and parsing? The browser is eating the competition's lunch.

The future of developer tooling isn't a download. It's a URL.
