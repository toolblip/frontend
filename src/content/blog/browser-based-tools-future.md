There's a quiet revolution happening in how developers use tools. Less downloading, less installing, less "wait 3 minutes for the Electron app to boot." More doing.

Browser-based tools — like the ones we build at Toolblip — are gaining ground fast. And for good reason.

## Privacy First

When you run a local app to format your JSON or hash a string, that data often goes somewhere. Maybe it's telemetry. Maybe it's just in memory longer than you'd like. With browser-based tools, your data never leaves your machine. Everything runs in your browser's sandbox. Close the tab, and it's gone.

No server round-trips. No logs. No "we promise we don't store this." Just privacy by architecture.

## No Install, No Hassle

Want to try a new tool? You click a link. That's it. No `brew install`. No dmg mounting. No "restart required." Whether you're on a loaner laptop, a ChromeOS device, or someone else's MacBook, you open the browser and you're productive immediately.

This matters more than we admit. The friction of installing software kills flow. Browser tools eliminate that friction entirely.

## Speed That Local Apps Can't Match

Here's the thing about most "native" developer tools — they're making network requests anyway. Your JSON formatter calls an API. Your "local" clipboard manager syncs to the cloud. The browser tools are often *faster* because they skip the middleman entirely.

Plus, updates are instant. The tool you see today is the tool you get. No version hunting, no "please update to 2.4.1."

## The Real Win

It's not just convenience. It's trust. Browser-based tools let you inspect every line of code, verify there's no data exfiltration, and run everything in an environment you control. That's not a small thing in a world where supply chain attacks and telemetry scandals are monthly news.

The future of developer tooling is open, local, and fast. Your browser's already the most powerful app on your machine. Might as well use it.
