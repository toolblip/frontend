You need to resize an image for a project. The old approach: upload it to some website, wait for the server to process it, download the result, hope the terms of service don't claim ownership of your photo. Not great.

There's a better way.

## Why Upload When Your Browser Can Do It?

Modern browsers have surprisingly powerful image processing capabilities. Canvas API, WebP encoding, File API — combine these and you have a full-featured image editor running at native speed, entirely on your machine.

No upload. No server. No "your image may be used for marketing purposes" checkbox.

## Crop and Resize Without Losing Quality

The key to good image resizing is doing it right. Using the browser's Canvas API, you can crop to exact pixel dimensions or scale proportionally. Want a 200×200 thumbnail from a landscape photo? Pick the area you want, get the result instantly.

Lossy compression settings let you dial in the quality-to-size tradeoff. A 4MB photo can become 80KB for web use with imperceptible quality loss.

## Format Conversion in Seconds

JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF — different formats have different strengths. WebP is smaller for web use. PNG preserves transparency. AVIF offers the best compression overall but has limited browser support.

With a browser-based converter, you can experiment with different formats and compare file sizes before committing. No trial-and-error uploads.

## Privacy: The Hidden Benefit

Here's the thing people don't think about: when you upload an image to "optimize" it on a random website, you're trusting that company with whatever's in that image. Personal photos. Business documents. Screenshots with sensitive information.

Browser-only processing means your images never leave your device. The tool doesn't store them, doesn't analyze them, doesn't use them to train models. They exist on your screen and nowhere else.
