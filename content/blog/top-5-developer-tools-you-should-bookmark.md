Every developer has a handful of tools they reach for multiple times a day. Not the big IDEs or CI pipelines — the small utilities that just do one thing well. Here are five that should be in everyone's bookmark bar.

## 1. JSON Formatter

Debugging messy JSON is a daily task. A good formatter validates your JSON, pretty-prints it with proper indentation, highlights syntax errors, and lets you minify it for production. The difference between staring at a wall of text and actually reading your data.

**[Try the JSON Formatter →](/tools/json-formatter)**

## 2. Base64 Encoder/Decoder

Encoding images into data URIs, passing binary data through JSON, handling API authentication headers — Base64 comes up constantly. A fast, reliable encoder/decoder that works entirely in your browser is non-negotiable.

**[Try Base64 Encoder/Decoder →](/tools/base64-encoder)**

## 3. Regex Tester

Writing regex without testing it is an exercise in optimism. A good regex tester shows you exactly what your pattern matches, highlights capture groups, explains what each part of the pattern does, and handles different regex flavors. Saves hours of trial and error.

**[Try Regex Tester →](/tools/regex-tester)**

## 4. Cron Expression Parser

Cron expressions are notoriously hard to read. `*/15 9-17 * * 1-5` — every developer pauses on that for a second. A parser that converts cron into human-readable descriptions and shows the next N run times is worth its weight in gold when debugging scheduled jobs.

**[Try Cron Parser →](/tools/cron-parser)**

## 5. Hash Generator

Generating MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256, or other hashes for files and strings comes up more often than you'd expect — verifying downloads, checking file integrity, working with APIs that require signed requests. A drag-and-drop hash generator that processes files locally is fast and private.

**[Try Hash Generator →](/tools/hash-generator)**
