There's a quiet revolution happening in how developers use tools. The desktop app — that bulky, always-outdated piece of software you downloaded in 2019 and forgot to update — is losing its grip. Browser-based tools are taking over, and honestly, it's hard to argue against them.

## Privacy by Design

When you run a tool in your browser, your data never leaves your machine. No server round-trips, no accounts to create, no telemetry to worry about. Paste a JSON payload containing an API key? That data is yours and yours alone. Compare that to online "free" tools that quietly log everything you process. The browser sandbox is a genuine privacy win — one that matters more every year.

## Zero Install, Instant Access

Search for "JSON formatter" and you'll find a dozen tools that work right now, in this tab, in two seconds. No `brew install`. No waiting for a 200MB download. No "requires macOS 13 or later" warnings. Your tools are available from any device with a browser. Switch from your Mac to a Windows VM to your phone — your tools are right there, same as before.

## Speed That Desktop Apps Can't Match

A browser tool loads in milliseconds because it's just HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. There's no splash screen, no "loading workspace", no background updater hogging your RAM. The UI is instant because there's no native layer between you and the code running.

## Always Current

No manually updating. No "new version available" dialogs. The tool is the latest version the moment the developer pushes it. Compare that to desktop apps where outdated versions can linger for months.

## The Tradeoffs Are Shrinking

Yes, browser tools historically struggled with heavy computation and offline access. But with WebAssembly, Service Workers, and localStorage, those gaps are closing fast. The remaining advantages of desktop apps are fewer every year.
