There's a quiet revolution happening in developer tooling. The apps you used to download, install, and update are increasingly living inside a browser tab. And honestly? It's about time.

## Privacy by Default

When you run a tool in your browser, your data never leaves your machine. No server calls, no telemetry, no "we promise we won't look at your JSON." Paste a password hash, encode a secret, process a screenshot—it's all happening locally. Even if the tool makes a server roundtrip (for things like OCR or AI parsing), the raw data stays under your control.

Compare that to the average desktop app, which often phones home on launch.

## No Install, No Hassle

Forget `.dmg` files, Homebrew installs, or "which version of Node do I need again?" With browser tools, you open a link and you're working. Share a URL with a colleague and they're up and running in seconds. No onboarding, no configuration, no "sudo make me a sandwich."

Updates are invisible—the tool simply refreshes. You always have the latest version without lifting a finger.

## Speed That Desktop Apps Can't Match

This one surprises people. But think about it: opening a browser tab is faster than launching a native app. Browser tools load near-instantly, use minimal memory compared to Electron apps, and work identically across macOS, Windows, and Linux.

For quick, one-off tasks—formatting JSON, decoding Base64, checking a regex—launching a full desktop app feels like firing up a tank to cross the street.
