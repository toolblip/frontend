There's a quiet revolution happening in developer tooling — and it starts with a URL bar.

Browser-based tools are eating the lunch of desktop apps and paid SaaS subscriptions alike. Not because they're trendy, but because they solve real problems that have been festering for years.

**Privacy isn't optional anymore.** When you upload a file to an online converter, who else is reading it? With a browser-based tool, your data never leaves your machine. The processing happens in JavaScript, right there in the tab. No server round-trip. No third-party access. Just you and the code.

**Zero install. Zero friction.** Need to debug a JWT at 11 PM? You don't open the App Store, create an account, download 200 MB, wait for installation, then restart your machine. You open a tab and you're working in seconds. Browser tools are bookmarks, not installations. They work on your laptop, your work desktop, yourChromebook, your iPad — anywhere with a browser.

**Speed hits different.** Native apps have overhead. Browser tools don't. A well-built web tool launches in milliseconds, handles most tasks instantly, and doesn't consume 500 MB of RAM just sitting idle. For common developer workflows — formatting JSON, testing regex, generating hashes — there's no need for a heavyweight desktop app.

**Always up to date.** No manual updates, no version drift between team members, no "sorry that feature was in v2.3." The tool updates itself every time you open it.

**Collaboration-ready by default.** Share a URL. No one needs to install anything to view the same tool output. Compare notes on a base64 decode, collaborate on a regex pattern, share a UUID — all through a link.
