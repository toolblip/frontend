Every developer has a mental list of tools they use dozens of times a week. These are ours — the five we open before almost anything else. And the best part? None of them require an install.

## 1. JSON Formatter

Debugging minified JSON is a nightmare. A good JSON formatter does two things: pretty-prints your data and validates the structure at the same time. If the JSON is invalid, you find out immediately instead of scratching your head through a silent API failure. [Try Toolblip's JSON Formatter →](/tools/json-formatter)

## 2. Base64 Encoder/Decoder

Base64 comes up constantly — encoding images for data URIs, handling API auth headers, working with tokens. The online encoders are often clunky or full of ads. A clean, local browser-based encoder processes your data without sending it anywhere. [Try Toolblip's Base64 Encoder →](/tools/base64)

## 3. Regex Tester

Writing a regex without testing it is a recipe for surprises. A good regex tester shows you matches in real time, explains what your pattern is doing, and highlights capture groups clearly. It's the difference between "I'm pretty sure this works" and "I know this works." [Try Toolblip's Regex Tester →](/tools/regex-tester)

## 4. Cron Expression Parser

Cron syntax is famously cryptic. `*/5 9-17 * * 1-5` — oh, that's every 5 minutes during business hours on weekdays. A cron parser that explains expressions in plain English saves you from misreading your own deployment schedule. [Try Toolblip's Cron Parser →](/tools/cron-parser)

## 5. Hash Generator

MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256 — sometimes you just need to hash something and you don't want to drop into a terminal. A browser-based hash generator does it instantly, locally, with zero friction. [Try Toolblip's Hash Generator →](/tools/hash-generator)

## The common thread

All five of these tools share something important: they process data client-side. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, nothing touches a server. Your data stays on your machine.

Bookmark them. You'll use them more than you think.
