Some tools you use once and forget. Others become part of your daily workflow so deeply you couldn't imagine working without them. These five are in the second category for me.

## 1. JSON Formatter & Validator

JSON is everywhere, but raw JSON is unreadable. A good formatter takes that wall of text and makes it scannable — indented, syntax-highlighted, and validated in one click.

Why it matters: Debugging a 500-line JSON response without formatting is a waste of time. Format it first, understand the structure, then dig in.

## 2. Base64 Encoder / Decoder

Encoding images to embed in CSS, decoding an Authorization header, translating binary data for API payloads — Base64 shows up constantly.

Why it matters: Doing this manually via code or a terminal command is friction. A fast encoder/decoder that handles files, strings, and URLs in one place eliminates that friction entirely.

## 3. Regex Tester

Regular expressions are powerful and terrifying in equal measure. A good regex tester shows you matches in real-time as you type, highlights groups, and explains what your pattern is actually doing.

Why it matters: Writing regex blind is a good way to produce patterns that work on your test input and nothing else. See matches live, catch edge cases early.

## 4. Cron Expression Parser

Cron syntax is compact and cryptic. `*/15 9-17 * * 1-5` — wait, is that business hours or something else? A parser that translates cron into human-readable schedules saves you from second-guessing yourself.

Why it matters: Misreading a cron schedule in production is a great way to discover you were firing a job every 15 minutes instead of every 15 hours at 3am. Parse before you deploy.

## 5. Hash Generator (MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256, etc.)

Verifying file integrity, comparing checksums, generating password or token hashes — you need a hash tool that supports the major algorithms and handles text and files equally well.

Why it matters: Most hash tools online only do one algorithm or require you to upload a file. A browser-based tool that does MD5 through SHA-512, locally, without uploading anything, is a privacy win on top of being convenient.

Bookmark these five and you'll be surprised how often your browser tabs shift from "trying to figure this out" to "got it in seconds."
