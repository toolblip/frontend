You need to resize a profile photo, convert a PNG to WebP, or crop something before uploading. Your instinct might be to open Figma, or hop onto a free image tool that promises "fast and secure." But here's the catch — most of those services upload your file to their servers first. Your image, their machine.

That's not always a problem. Until it is.

## Why Browser-Only Processing Matters

When you process an image in your browser using client-side JavaScript (Canvas API, specifically), the file never leaves your device. No server in the middle. No logs of your upload. No "we may retain this file for service improvement" buried in a privacy policy you didn't read.

This is especially important when you're working with:
- Screenshots with names, faces, or sensitive UI
- Business documents
- Images from private projects
- Anything you'd rather not send to a third party

## What You Can Actually Do Client-Side

The browser is more capable than most people realize:

**Resize & Crop** — Draw the image to a canvas at your target dimensions. Crop to a specific aspect ratio. No server involved.

**Format Conversion** — Convert between PNG, JPEG, WebP, and more. WebP alone can shrink file sizes by 25-35% compared to JPEG at similar quality.

**Compression** — Adjust quality settings and see the file size drop in real-time before downloading.

**Metadata Stripping** — Remove EXIF data that can reveal location, device info, or timestamps you didn't mean to share.

## How It Works Under the Hood

When you drop an image into a browser-based tool, JavaScript reads it as a data URL or ArrayBuffer. The Canvas API takes it from there — drawing, resampling, and exporting as a new file. The original never gets serialized and sent anywhere. Your browser is the entire pipeline.

## When to Use a Native App Instead

Browser tools handle anything under a few megapixels instantly. Processing a 50-megapixel RAW file, though? That's going to be slow in a tab and might even crash your browser. For heavy lifting, native apps still win.

But for the daily image tasks — resize for web, convert for a specific format, strip metadata before sharing — browser-based tools have caught up. And with privacy increasingly hard to assume, processing locally is the smart default.
