Image optimization usually means one of two things: uploading to a third-party service and waiting, or downloading heavyweight desktop software. There's a better way — and it's been in your browser this whole time.

## Why Local Processing Wins

When you upload an image to "optimize" it, you're sending your data to someone else's server. That means:

- Your image data leaves your machine
- It might be stored, logged, or used to train models
- You're dependent on their servers being up
- Upload times scale with file size

Local browser-based processing has none of these problems. Your images never leave your device. The processing happens on your GPU via WebGL or WebAssembly. And once you're done, the optimized file is ready to download immediately.

## What You Can Do Locally

Modern browser APIs are surprisingly powerful for image manipulation:

**Cropping** — Select a region, set exact pixel dimensions, maintain aspect ratio. Drag-and-drop or input precise measurements.

**Resizing** — Scale images down for web use, generate thumbnails, or resize for specific platforms. Good tools preserve quality with smart interpolation.

**Format Conversion** — Convert between PNG, JPEG, WebP, and AVIF. Each format has trade-offs; a local converter lets you experiment quickly.

**Compression** — Reduce file size without noticeable quality loss. Some tools let you set a target file size or quality level and preview the result.

**Metadata Stripping** — Remove EXIF data, GPS locations, camera info. Useful for privacy before sharing online.

## Choosing the Right Tool

Look for tools that process entirely in-browser. Check that they support drag-and-drop, show preview before download, and let you adjust quality/size interactively. The best ones feel like desktop apps but load in milliseconds.
