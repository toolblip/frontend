Image optimization is one of those tasks that usually forces a trade-off: either use a desktop app (heavy, installed) or upload to a web service (convenient but a privacy gamble). What if you could get the convenience of a web tool with the privacy guarantees of a desktop app? That's exactly what browser-based image processing delivers.

## Why "no upload" matters more than people think

When you upload an image to a web service, you're trusting that company with whatever's in that image. Maybe it's a screenshot with sensitive customer data. Maybe it's a photo with GPS metadata. Maybe it's nothing sensitive — but do you really want to find out later that the service was logging everything?

Client-side image processing eliminates this entirely. The image never leaves your device. The browser does all the work.

## What you can do entirely in-browser

Modern web APIs give browser-based tools serious image editing muscle:

- **Crop and resize** — Drag to select a region, set exact dimensions, maintain aspect ratio
- **Format conversion** — Convert between PNG, JPEG, WebP, and other formats directly in the tab
- **Compression** — Reduce file size with adjustable quality settings, see the results instantly
- **Metadata stripping** — Remove EXIF data (camera info, GPS coordinates, timestamps) before sharing
- **Color adjustments** — Tweak brightness, contrast, saturation without opening Photoshop

All of this happens on your machine. No server round-trip. No waiting for an upload to complete. No file size limits based on someone's server costs.

## Performance is better than you'd expect

You might assume that processing a 10MB image in a browser tab would choke. With WebAssembly and modern browser APIs, it's surprisingly smooth. The image loads into a `<canvas>` element, gets processed in-memory, and gets exported. It's fast — often faster than launching a native app.

## No sign-up, no storage, no tracking

The best browser-based image tools don't require an account, don't store your images on their servers (because they literally can't — the processing is client-side), and don't show ads. They're utilities in the truest sense: they do one job, do it well, and stay out of your way.
