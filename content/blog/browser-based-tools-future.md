There's a quiet revolution happening in developer tooling. The apps you used to download, install, and update are increasingly living inside a browser tab. And honestly? That's a good thing.

## Privacy You Can Actually Trust

When you run a tool locally in your browser, your data never leaves your machine. No server, no third party, no "we promise we're not looking at your files." You can disconnect from the internet entirely and the tool still works. Try doing that with an Electron app that phones home on startup.

This matters especially for sensitive work — encoding tokens, parsing config files with credentials, or processing data you're not ready to share. With browser-based tools, the worst-case scenario is... nothing happens. Your data stays on your device.

## Zero Install Overhead

Downloading a 200MB app to decode a Base64 string once a month? No thanks. Browser tools load in milliseconds and are always up to date. No auto-updater interrupting your flow. No disk space consumed. No "a new version is available" badge staring at you from the dock.

And if you're on a new machine or a restricted work laptop? You still have your tools. Just open a bookmark.

## Speed That Desktop Apps Can't Match

Browser engines are heavily optimized. JavaScript execution is fast — faster than many people assume. For typical developer tasks like JSON formatting, regex testing, or hash generation, the browser handles thousands of operations per second without breaking a sweat.

The startup time is also effectively zero. There's no splash screen, no license check, no module loading. Open tab, paste, done.

## The Tradeoffs Are Shrinking

Yes, browser tools historically had limits — no filesystem access, no native integrations, poor offline support. But Web APIs have closed most of those gaps. The File System Access API, background sync, and progressive web app capabilities mean modern browser tools can do things that would've seemed impossible five years ago.

The future of developer tooling isn't a thicker app. It's a lighter tab.
