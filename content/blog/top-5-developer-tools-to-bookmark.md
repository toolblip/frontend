Every developer has a ritual: the tab-cluttered browser, the half-dozen Stack Overflow windows, the 3 AM scramble to remember the cron syntax for "every other Tuesday." These five tools won't solve everything, but they'll make the everyday annoyances disappear.

## 1. JSON Formatter & Validator

You've got a 200-line JSON blob in your clipboard. It looks wrong but you can't find the bug. A good JSON formatter pretty-prints the whole thing, highlights structural errors, and lets you collapse/expand nodes. Paste → validate → fix → copy. No more squinting.

## 2. Base64 Encoder / Decoder

Encoding API keys for Authorization headers, embedding small images as data URIs, decoding a Base64 payload from a webhook — this comes up more than you'd think. Doing it in JavaScript manually or with a sketchy online encoder is fine until you accidentally paste something sensitive into a third-party site. Browser-only processing means nothing leaves your machine.

## 3. Regex Tester

Writing a regex without testing it is how bugs are born. A solid regex tester lets you write a pattern, throw test strings at it, see match groups highlighted in real-time, and understand why your `[^,]+` is eating the wrong thing. Saves at least one "why isn't this working" spiral per week.

## 4. Cron Expression Parser

Cron is famously unreadable. `*/15 9-17 * * 1-5` — that's every quarter hour during business hours on weekdays, right? A cron parser confirms this and shows you the next N execution times. Indispensable when you're scheduling something important and want to sanity-check before deploying.

## 5. Hash Generator

MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256, SHA-512 — sometimes you just need to verify a checksum. Generating a hash locally in the browser is faster than reaching for a terminal, and again, your file never goes anywhere.
